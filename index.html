🔴 HIGH IMPACT — Must Act
Massachusetts Money Transmitter License — COMPLIANCE DEADLINE PASSED

What: M.G.L. c. 169B (Money Transmission Law) effective Jan 1, 2026. Hard money lenders must obtain money transmitter licenses.

Impact: Deadline was July 1, 2026. Any lender who did not comply by that date must immediately stop lending in MA until licensed.

Action: If hard money lending in MA: (1) Verify license status immediately; (2) If not licensed, do not originate new loans in MA; (3) Contact MA Division of Banks to obtain/maintain license; (4) Document compliance.

HIGH Hard Money Compliance
OBBBA: 100% Bonus Depreciation Now Permanent (IRS Notice 2026-11/16)

What: The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (signed July 4, 2025) made 100% bonus depreciation permanent for qualified property acquired after Jan 19, 2025. IRS issued Notice 2026-11 (general guidance) and Notice 2026-16 (special depreciation rules).

Impact: Massive boost to real estate depreciation deductions. Cost segregation studies now extremely valuable—100% first-year deductions on 5/7/15-year assets.

Action: For all 2025+ acquisitions: (1) Consider cost seg studies (especially for commercial/multifamily); (2) Plan for large depreciation deductions; (3) Update tax projections; (4) Consult on bonus depreciation elections.

HIGH Real Estate Federal Tax
Massachusetts Decouples from Federal Bonus Depreciation (2025-2026)

What: Massachusetts temporarily decouples from federal special depreciation allowance (IRC §168(n)) for qualified production property (QPP) placed in service in 2025 and 2026 tax years.

Impact: STATE-FEDERAL MISMATCH: Federal return claims 100% bonus depreciation, but MA return requires normal MACRS depreciation. Creates a permanent state add-back.

Action: For MA residents: (1) Track federal vs. state depreciation schedules separately; (2) Plan for permanent state add-backs on QPP; (3) Consult on alternative minimum tax (AMT) exposure; (4) Consider amended returns if already filed 2025 with federal bonus dep.

MEDIUM Real Estate MA Tax
🟡 MEDIUM IMPACT — Plan Ahead
Massachusetts Surtax Threshold Increased to $1,107,750

What: The 4% surtax threshold (MA "millionaire tax") increased from $1,083,150 to $1,107,750 for all filing statuses, effective Jan 1, 2026. Indexed annually for inflation.

Impact: Income above $1,107,750 = 9% state tax instead of 5%. Affects W-2 wages, business income, and capital gains.

Action: For high-income business owners: (1) Review 2026 projected income; (2) Consider 1040 withholding adjustments if threshold will be crossed; (3) Plan timing of large real estate sales or loan payoffs; (4) Evaluate QBI deduction impact.

MEDIUM MA Tax Tax Planning
Opportunity Zones 2.0 Made Permanent (Effective Jan 1, 2027)

What: The OBBBA made the Opportunity Zone program permanent and updated it to Opportunity Zones 2.0, effective Jan 1, 2027. SALT deduction cap increased to $40,000 (2025-2029).

Impact: OZ investments now have permanent incentive; $40k SALT cap relief for real estate investors.

Action: For eligible investments: (1) Evaluate OZ 2.0 benefits for 2026-year acquisitions (take effect 1/1/27); (2) Take advantage of $40k SALT cap through 2029.

MEDIUM Real Estate QOZ
2026 Section 179 Expensing Limits Confirmed

What: Section 179 expensing limit is $2,560,000 for 2026; phase-out threshold is $4,090,000. No changes from 2025.

Impact: Carwash equipment and hard money lending infrastructure can be expensed up to $2.56M in 2026.

Action: For equipment purchases in 2026: (1) Plan Section 179 elections in tandem with 100% bonus depreciation; (2) Track total QIP placed in service.
