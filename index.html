New: ROAD to Housing Act — Institutional Investor Moratorium
New — Jul 2026
  • What: The 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act (H.R. 6644) became law July 11, 2026 — the first major bipartisan federal housing package since the 1990s (Senate 85–5, House 358–32). It imposes the first broad federal moratorium on large institutional investors purchasing single-family homes, alongside dozens of supply-side reforms (zoning incentives, permitting, manufactured housing). The final text softened the build-to-rent provisions: the 7-year forced-disposal requirement and renter right-of-first-refusal were dropped.
  • Impact on you: You are not the target — the moratorium aims at large Wall Street-scale buyers, not local investors. Net effect is likely favorable: your best-capitalized competition for single-family acquisitions is restricted, which could modestly ease bidding pressure on flip and rental purchases in your MA/CT markets. Your hard money borrowers (local flippers) benefit the same way.
  • Strategy: Watch for implementing regulations defining "large institutional investor" thresholds to confirm your entities fall well outside the definition (they should). If institutional demand pulls back in any of your submarkets, be ready to acquire — less competition at the margin can mean better entry prices. No compliance action expected for your scale.

New: MRVP/AHVP Voucher Rent-Increase Rules
New — Jul 2026
  • What: Effective July 1, 2026, updated rules for the Massachusetts Rental Voucher Program (MRVP) and Alternative Housing Voucher Program (AHVP). Contract rents may only be increased at the lease-renewal date, increases must be requested at least 90 days before renewal, and any request made fewer than 60 days out will not be processed until the following year.
  • Impact on you: Applies to any residential unit rented to a voucher-holding tenant. Missing the 90-day window means a full year with no rent increase on that unit.
  • Strategy: Add a 90-days-before-renewal reminder for every voucher tenancy. Submit increase requests early and confirm receipt with the administering agency. No effect on market-rate (non-voucher) units.

New: PTET Election Now Covers the 4% Surtax
New — Jul 2026
  • What: Massachusetts' FY2026 supplemental budget (H.5470, enacted June 12, 2026) expands the elective pass-through entity excise (PTET) to include income subject to the 4% surtax, effective for tax years beginning in 2026. Previously the PTET workaround covered only the base 5% income tax. The same law delays MA conformity to certain OBBBA business provisions (§179 expensing, R&D/§174A, business-interest §163(j)) until state revenue triggers are met.
  • Impact on you: In years when flip gains, rental income, or lending profits push your taxable income above $1,107,750, an electing S-corp or partnership can now pay the surtax portion at the entity level — making it federally deductible without regard to the SALT cap (members claim the 90% MA credit as usual). Separately, MA may not follow federal treatment of §179 and business-interest deductions on your state return — federal/state books can diverge.
  • Strategy: Have your CPA model a 2026 PTET election that includes the surtax before Q3 estimated payments (due Sept 15). Confirm which entities benefit most — the election is made per-entity annually. Flag the OBBBA conformity delay so MA depreciation/interest add-backs don't surprise you at filing time.

QOZ Transitional Guidance — IRS Notice 2026-40
New — Jun 18, 2026
  • What: The IRS issued the first transitional guidance reconciling the old TCJA Opportunity Zone rules with the OBBBA rewrite (ahead of proposed regulations). It addresses how investments in previously designated zones are treated as the original zone designations wind down (expiring Dec 31, 2028 for most zones; Dec 31, 2027 for Puerto Rico).
  • Key points: (1) Pre-2027 deferred gains in a QOF are recognized on Dec 31, 2026 and cannot be re-deferred — but the investment stays eligible for the 10-year FMV basis step-up on later sale. (2) Two safe harbors let you keep buying property in older zones after 2026: a working-capital plan exception (plan adopted by Dec 31, 2026, with 10% funded / 5% spent) and an ordinary-course replacement exception (modernization/replacement of existing assets — not expansion). (3) A compliance bridge keeps expired zones treated as QOZs through Dec 31, 2047 for the substantial-use and 50% gross-income tests.
  • Strategy: If you hold any QOF interests, model the Dec 31, 2026 income inclusion into year-end liquidity now. If you're developing in an existing zone, get a written working-capital plan adopted and partially funded before year-end 2026 to preserve QOZBP status on post-2026 purchases. For new gains, weigh deferring the investment to on/after Jan 1, 2027 to access the OBBBA 5-year inclusion deferral and 10% (30% rural) basis step-up. New 2027-cycle zone designations are due from states by July 1, 2026.

MA Rent Control Ballot Initiative — Struck From Ballot
Risk Removed — Jun 23
  • What: On June 23, 2026 the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court ruled the rent-control question cannot appear on the November 2026 ballot. Although the Elections Division had certified 88,132 valid signatures (well above the ~74,574 required), the Court found the petition unconstitutional because it carved out an exemption for units "operated solely for … religious … purposes" — and the MA Constitution bars initiative petitions "relating to religion, religious practices, or religious institutions." The measure would have capped annual rent increases at inflation (max 5%), exempting new construction (10 years) and owner-occupied buildings of 4 or fewer units.
  • Impact: The single biggest near-term risk to your residential rental portfolio is off the table for 2026 — no statewide rent cap will go before voters this November. However, supporters (Homes for All Massachusetts) called the religious-exemption flaw "easily fixable" and intend to redraft and refile, so a similar measure could return via a future ballot cycle or the legislature.
  • Strategy: Risk is deferred, not eliminated. You can stand down any defensive rent resets that were driven purely by ballot-timing urgency, but keep rents at market and document increases. Continue monitoring Homes for All and the legislature's rent-stabilization bills (still in Joint Committee). Maintain MassLandlords membership for early warning on any refiled initiative.

  • Strategy: Watch what passes by July 31. Ask your chemical suppliers whether any detergents, waxes, or sealants used at the carwash contain intentionally added PFAS, and start identifying PFAS-free alternatives — getting ahead of a ban is cheaper than scrambling after one.

MA Usury Laws — 20% Criminal Threshold
Compliance
  • What: Massachusetts prescribes 6% legal interest unless agreed otherwise in writing. Charging above 20% per annum triggers criminal usury penalties (up to 10 years imprisonment, $10K fine).

  • Strategy: Calculate your all-in effective rate including points and fees on every loan to ensure you stay below 20%. Have your attorney review fee structures. For higher-risk loans, use equity participation or profit-sharing instead of higher interest rates.

Risk
